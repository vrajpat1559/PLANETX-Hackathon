How it works

  1. Pick a spot. Type a star's name or coordinates, or try one of the examples.
  2. Line up the photos. SPHEREx images of that spot from each visit are aligned and combined, with older WISE images going back to 2010.
  3. Flip between them. Press Play, or step with ← →. Switch on Highlight to colour what changed.

What to look for

  • A dot that walks. A nearby star moving through space. Barnard's Star crosses the width of the full Moon in under 200 years.
  • A dot that brightens or dims. A variable star. R Draconis swells and dims over about eight months.
  • Something there once, then gone. An asteroid passing through, or a cosmic ray hitting the detector.

About SPHEREx and the data

SPHEREx launched in March 2025. Every six months it maps the whole sky in 102 infrared colours, from 0.75 to 5 microns, to study how the universe expanded after the Big Bang, how galaxies grew, and where the water and ice for new planets comes from.

Images come straight from NASA's archive (IRSA at Caltech/IPAC), read from its public copy on Amazon for speed. New SPHEREx images are published every week and show up here automatically. The older history comes from NASA's WISE and NEOWISE missions, 2010 to 2024.
