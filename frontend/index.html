What is blinking?

The same sky, on different nights.

SPHEREx photographs the whole sky over and over. Blinking flips between pictures of one spot taken on different dates. Stars and galaxies stay put, so anything that moves, brightens or fades appears to jump.

3 What to look for

  • A dot that hops from frame to frame is something moving: an asteroid, a comet, or a nearby fast star.
  • A dot that appears, fades or pulses may be a variable star or an outburst. If it shows up in just one frame, it is more likely a cosmic ray hitting the detector.
  • Gentle flicker everywhere is normal. SPHEREx records each exposure in a slightly different infrared colour, so not every change is a change over time.

Astronomers have hunted this way for a century: Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto in 1930 by blinking two photographic plates.

North is up and east is left in every frame. One SPHEREx pixel covers 6.15 arcseconds of sky. Built for NASA Space Apps 2026.
